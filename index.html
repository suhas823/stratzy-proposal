Stratzy Complaint Audit

Stratzy has largely fixed its app. Login, crash and broker complaints have almost disappeared. What users complain about now is money: the gap between what the app shows and what lands in their account, algos that don't execute as designed, and losses deeper than they expected.

Complaints have moved from app problems to money and trust

Share of complaints mentioning each problem, comparing 2021–24 (198 complaints) with 2025–26 (72). Lines show 95% Wilson intervals. One complaint can mention several problems.

2021–24 2025–26 clear shift intervals don't overlap

Three shifts are clear even with only 72 recent complaints: crashes and lag fell, while "risk worse than expected" and "can't understand the app" rose. The other rising themes point the same way, but their intervals still overlap.

A question I'd explore

When a user says the app's numbers don't match their account, 72% of replies explain market risk, for example that profit and loss are inherent to markets. I'd want to know whether these users are asking about risk, or asking how their own result was calculated. If it's the second, a reply that walks through their actual trades may land better.

One burst of praise is set aside

115 of the 508 praise reviews arrived in one quarter, July to September 2024. That batch contains 66 of the 79 reviews ending in a double full stop, and almost all the "AI algorithms are top-notch" praise. This audit doesn't claim those reviews are fake. It sets them aside when describing what users value, so the overall star rating is a weak guide on its own.

Outside that batch, 393 praise reviews mostly mention ease of use (30%) and the quality of ideas and research (24%). Only 15% mention returns or profit.

What this points to

Two problems a product intern could own, both outside the approved algo logic. Both are written up in the My Results PRD.

Show users their own fills

Put the algo's model trades next to what actually executed in the user's broker account: missed orders, entry price gap, slippage, fees. The dashboard stops being a claim the user has to trust.

Covers: numbers don't match · didn't execute · trust

Price the risk before deploying

Before a user deploys, show the algo's worst historical drawdown in rupees on their own capital, the stop-loss level, and whether losses can trigger a fund top-up. Surprise is the complaint, not the loss.

Covers: worse than expected · can't understand

Check with compliance before letting users set their own loss caps. Under SEBI's retail algo framework, a change to an approved algo needs fresh exchange approval, and a user-set cap may count as one.
